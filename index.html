Investigation: Willys grew 1.0%, The market grew 3.7%

For two consecutive quarters, Willys has grown at a slower rate than the Swedish grocery market. Before drawing conclusions, I decided to investigate four aspects that would influence this rate: a strong year before, falling prices, the food VAT cut, and stores closed for rebuilds. This note goes through what the public record shows, and a few analyses that could be interesting to try with Willys Plus data.

This is an outside-in view built only from public reports. With no access to internal data, it is simply a set of questions I found interesting.

What The Public Numbers Say

Growth Has Slowed Down, But Volume Has NotAxfood describes recent growth as volume-driven in a market with food price deflation.
Profit Is Holding, While Margin Is SqueezedWillys Q1 2026 operating margin was 4.1%, down from 4.3%, on SEK 498m operating profit.
The Loyalty Base Is LargeWillys Plus has passed 4 million members, across a network of about 257 stores.

Willys beat the market by 4.1 points in Q2 2025, then fell behind by 0.6 points in Q1 2026 and 2.7 points in Q2 2026. Management points to three drivers: a very high base from last year, price deflation, and, in Q1, two stores that were closed for refurbishment or relocation and would have added roughly one point of growth had they been open. On a like-for-like basis, Q2 was about minus 1%.

Looked at over two years, Willys is still slightly ahead of the market. That does not mean the slowdown can be ignored, but the headline gap may make it look bigger than it is. A sensible first step would be to work out how much of the gap is base effect, and how much reflects a change in customer behaviour.

Why The Price Environment Complicates The Reading

On 1 April 2026, Sweden cut food VAT from 12% to 6%, in place until 31 December 2027. If a shelf price rose or fell only because of VAT, the consumer price would move by (1.06 ÷ 1.12 − 1) −5.4%. Retailers report sales excluding VAT, so a pure pass-through leaves revenue per unit unchanged, while shoppers see lower prices. Therefore, consumer price indices and reported sales are measuring different things, and a price-volume bridge has to be built on the right basis.

Two other aspects are significant. The government has set up a food price commission to monitor whether retailers pass the cut on, and in July it opened a further inquiry into a more selective VAT model. And because the cut expires at the end of 2027, unless there is an extension, there is a one-off price step up in January 2028. Monitoring customer behaviourleading up to and around that date might be worth exploring in good time.

How I Approach My Qork

I like to begin by talking with the people who own the question, so that we agree on the decision the analysis should support. From there I write down what we expect to see before opening the data, which makes it easier to notice when the numbers say something different. I do most first passes in SQL, use BI tools for views people return to, and turn to Python or R only where a model adds something. I aim to finish with a clear suggestion the team can discuss and improve together.
